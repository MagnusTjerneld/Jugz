The same three-vessel puzzle, poured into stemware. Water becomes red wine, jugs become differently tall wine glasses, and the unit reads U instead of L. The skin is not in a settings menu — it hides behind the logo. This board is the graphics draft: geometry, palette and wording, before a line of it touches index.html.

Three taps, 1.2 s window

A single tap would fire by accident and a menu entry would give the joke away. Three taps inside 1.2 s is discoverable by fiddling, invisible otherwise. The droplet rotates 180° on the way over — it is pouring, not toggling.

The wipe carries the change

A circle of --deep grows from the droplet over 620 ms; the skin swaps under it at 650 ms, then it fades. No cross-fading of a hundred properties, and the state change lands in one gesture.

Nothing else moves

Same level, same pours, same stars. The skin only rewrites tokens, the vessel path and the unit string — progress is untouched, so switching mid-level is safe.

Sticky, but quiet

The choice belongs in jugz_v1 next to current and stars. It survives a reload; it never appears in the UI.

02 — glassware

Three glasses, sized by capacity

Capacity drives four numbers at once: bowl height, bowl width, belly bulge and stem length. The smallest cap reads as a port tulip, the largest as a burgundy balloon — so the three glasses in a level are told apart by silhouette, not just by size.

Wine fills the bowl, never the stem

The fill band runs from the rim down to the bowl's bottom, not to the foot of the SVG. Reusing the jug's topY = 20, botY = H − 10 would pour wine into the stem. Each glass carries its own band; the clip path is the bowl outline alone.

Bubbles out, legs in

Rising bubbles read as fizz — wrong for still red. In their place, three faint tears run down the wall above the surface every 7–9 s, and stop at the wine line.

The rim is an ellipse

The jug is drawn flat-on. A glass needs the opening: a full rim ellipse, near edge at full stroke, far edge at 45 % — the one cue that makes the shape read as stemware rather than a cone.

The primary button needs its own token

Turquoise takes dark text (#0B2B26); ruby does not — at that lightness it drops to roughly 2:1. The skin adds --on-primary, light on wine, dark on water. The one place where a straight token swap is not enough.

06 — how it's wired

What the implementation actually costs

One attribute, two token blocks

document.documentElement.dataset.skin selects [data-skin="wine"], which redefines the same properties already in :root. The difficulty badges are the exception: their backgrounds are hardcoded rgba() literals today and need skin values too.

jugSVG() gains a sibling, not a branch

glassSVG() emits the same element ids (wateri, wrecti, surfgi) so render() is unchanged apart from reading the fill band off the SVG instead of assuming it.

The unit is a constant

Three sites print it — goal text, bowl etch, amount label. One UNIT lookup covers all three, plus the vessel noun in the goal sentence.

The shipped identity stays Jugz

Title, manifest, icons, og:image and the description are untouched — a skin nobody can see from the outside stays secret. That also keeps check.js green: it asserts on generated files, manifest sizes and og URLs, none of which move.

Reduced motion already covers it

Legs and the wipe live under the existing prefers-reduced-motion block; with motion off the swap is instantaneous and the wine sits still.
